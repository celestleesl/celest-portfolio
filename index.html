Case Study · @two.hungryhumans Ongoing since 2024

Building a followingfrom zero.

The Vision

A self-funded food page turned into a real testbed for growth marketing, no client budget, no existing audience, just consistent execution.

Role
Co-Founder & Content Lead
Duration
Jan 2024 - Present

"I started Two Hungry Humans as a passion project, and it became a real testbed for everything I know about marketing."

A food review page covering Singapore and Johor Bahru. No brief, no fixed budget, just me figuring it out: content strategy, platform growth, community building, and eventually a bit of paid amplification, all without the safety net of an existing audience or client budget behind me.

The Challenge
Growing an account from absolutely nothing.

Two things made early growth genuinely hard: figuring out what actually resonated, and building a system to keep producing without burning out. The ideas were never the bottleneck, getting from "good idea" to "posted" reliably was.

1Finding the right format, hook, and pacing with zero starting audience
2Producing consistently, solo, without burning out on filming and editing
3Turning "good idea" into "posted" reliably, week after week
What I did
Repeatable Workflow

Batched filming, a standardized editing style, and a templated caption structure, so I wasn't reinventing the process every time.

Tight Niche Focus

Singapore and JB food specifically, instead of trying to be a generic food page competing with everyone.

Format Iteration

Refining hooks and pacing across Instagram and TikTok based on what actually performed, not what I assumed would.

I owned the entire pipeline myself: strategy, filming, editing, copywriting, posting, replying to comments, and eventually managing a small ad budget, kept organic as the core strategy, but tested a small paid boost on one post to see what reach potential it could unlock.

Data & Impact
Growth that started from zero.

In roughly 3 months, reaching 24,902 unique viewers, 80.5% of whom weren't even following the page yet, which tells me the content itself was doing the work of pulling in new people, not just re-serving existing followers.

Static posts punch above their weight on interactions, which tells me people engage differently depending on format, something I want to lean into more deliberately. My top-performing post organically was a June 23rd piece that hit 15.6K views and 269 interactions on its own, no paid support. Four of my next best posts (13–9.3K, 5.9K, 2.9K, 2K views) came from a mix of formats, a good sign the page isn't a one-hit wonder.

Proof point: testing paid amplification

Separately, I ran one small paid test on my "I started a food blog: send help" reel: SGD 17.92 over 6 days. It drove 12,571 of that post's 16,027 total views, 8,587 reach, and 450 profile visits, at just $0.04 per profile visit. It also surfaced a useful audience insight I didn't expect: 95.5% of the paid reach was in Johor Bahru versus only 4.5% in Singapore, which is now shaping how I think about targeting JB content going forward.

I'm also now starting to field early media drop and brand collaboration offers as a direct result of the page's traction (details to come once those are confirmed).

What I'd do differently

My posting cadence still isn't as consistent as I'd like, so building a stricter content calendar is next on my list. I've also been going a lot on gut feel about what works. I want to get more rigorous about tracking performance data so decisions are backed by numbers, not just instinct. I'm also aware I lean heavily on one content format, so I want to test more variety (carousels, stories, behind-the-scenes) instead of defaulting to reels.

On a personal note, I'm working on getting more comfortable being on camera. It's something I actively want to do more of, and I think leaning into it will unlock a level of content (and connection with the audience) that scripted or edited posts can't fully replace.

Why it matters

This project shows I can grow a brand from near zero to over 60K views and inbound brand interest in about 3 months, using organic-first strategy, niche positioning, and small, targeted paid tests to actually learn something about the audience rather than just buy numbers. It's the same discipline (content strategy, performance analysis, efficient spend) that I bring to client work at Physio & Sole, AnjouHealth, and beyond.

Case Study · Physio & Sole Clinic Ongoing since 2024

Making medical marketingfeel human.

The Vision

A shoe-review video series that turns a clinic's podiatrist into a relatable, shareable voice, not a formal talking head.

Role
Marketing Executive
Duration
2024 - Present

"Our own podiatrist, reacting to and reviewing real shoes on camera, in a fun, relatable way instead of a formal clinical tone."

It started with my lady boss Fiona spotting a genuinely good, affordable school shoe at Decathlon and wanting to talk about it. That became "Hey Podiatrist, Can We Ask About Kids School Shoes? (Decathlon Edition)," which pulled 37.6K views. Around the same time she also filmed some casual clips on a Japan trip talking about shoes there, and that one hit 94K. Two solid results back to back was enough signal for the team to decide this format was worth building on properly.

The Challenge
Marketing that doesn't read as clinical.

Physio & Sole Clinic needed its marketing to feel less like a service listing and more like something patients would actually want to engage with, especially tricky in a medical space where content can easily read as dry or overly clinical.

1Keeping medical credibility intact while making the tone fun and shareable
2Turning early traction into a repeatable, brand-partnership-ready format
3Tuning hooks and pacing for virality without losing the clinic's voice
What I did
Format Development

Spotted the early traction on the Decathlon and Japan videos and pitched building it into an ongoing content series.

Brand Partnerships

Grew the format into a real pipeline, liaising directly with Decathlon, Bata, and FitFlop to get product in for review.

SEO & Content Strategy

Mapped keyword research to real patient search intent, collaborating directly with clinicians on blog content and internal linking.

I also deliberately leaned into a controversial angle on one video ("Sheena No-No-Shoes"), challenging the idea that the flip-flops most of us wore as kids were actually fine for our feet, while working with our podiatrist to keep the medical credibility intact.

Proof point: the breakout video

"Sheena No-No-Shoes" (posted April 2, 2025) is the account's clear high point: 146,378 reach, 193,519 views, a 5.39% engagement rate, and 1,120 new followers directly off that one post. A small follow-up ad boost in May added another 10,239 views and 142 followers for under $60.

Audience insight: kids vs. adults

Once the format was working, we tried extending it to adult shoe reviews, but that content didn't land nearly as hard. My read on why: parents are highly motivated by their kids' growth and development, so "is this shoe actually good for my child's feet" hits an emotional nerve. Adults tend to prioritize how a shoe looks over the wellness angle, so the same format doesn't carry the same urgency.

Brand partnerships

Beyond the viral hit, the format became a genuine partnership channel:

  • FitFlop's "SK FitFlop" review hit 23,370 views and drove +134 followers, and a follow-up profile-visit-goal ad ($59.95) converted at 3.52%, far outperforming an earlier "Ask A Pod" CTA ad on the same brand ($59.49, 10 clicks), a useful lesson in matching the ad objective to what audiences actually respond to.
  • Two Bata reviews (school shoes and sandals) added 27 and 31 followers respectively, with the sandal video's 5.3% engagement rate holding up well against the account average.
  • Hoka is shortlisted for an upcoming Hyrox-themed collab, in the pipeline rather than delivered yet.
Proof point: organic search performance

Two separate data sources back this up. Historically (Google Search Console, ~3 months), the clinic pulled roughly 17,000 legitimate organic clicks (after excluding a spam/hijack issue I flagged separately) with several patient-question keywords ranking #1. More recently, GA4 confirms organic search is the single highest-converting channel on the site: in the most recent tracked period, organic search drove 3,838 sessions, a 58.96% engagement rate, the longest average session duration of any channel (3m 03s), and 220 key events, 34.8% of all conversions on the site.

Beyond the video series

I also pick up ad hoc design work for the clinic: festive greeting cards, gifting stickers, and a full logo package (3D, flat, and sticker versions) for a new clinic location's shopfront.

On camera, and cross-brand work (WACC)

I'm also sometimes the face of the video content myself, not just behind the camera. I do the same kind of content for Women & Children Centre (WACC), which operates under the same clinic group as Physio & Sole but focuses specifically on women's and children's health, so content and strategy naturally cross over between the two brands. For WACC, the approach stays consistent with Physio & Sole: search-intent-led content planning, structured FAQs, and a tone calibrated to be reassuring and plain-spoken given the more sensitive subject matter.

I've also helped execute ALIGN, a career-focused event for physiotherapists run under AHA (Allied Health Academy, a sub-brand of the same group), handling event marketing plus on-the-ground logistics: taking attendance, and shooting photo and video on the day.

Results
  • One breakout video drove 193,519 views, 1,120 new followers, and the highest shares-to-likes ratio in the account's history.
  • Grew the account from 2,749 to 7,147 followers (+160%) in about 18 months, with growth clearly tied to specific content decisions, not just time passing.
  • Built a genuine brand partnership pipeline (Decathlon, Bata, FitFlop, with Hoka in discussion), and used performance data from those campaigns to improve ad targeting decisions.
  • Organic search is the top-converting channel on the website, driving more key events than paid search despite $0 media spend.
  • Flagged a technical SEO/security issue (spam-driven traffic skewing analytics) that needed attention beyond the marketing work itself.
Why it matters

This shows I can spot a content signal early and turn it into something bigger: a viral moment, a repeatable format, and an actual brand partnership engine, backed by real, verified data rather than inflated numbers. It also shows range across video strategy, paid social, SEO, and even catching technical issues that pure marketing work wouldn't usually touch.

Made possible by one good idea, on camera.

Case Study · AnjouHealth Ongoing

A confident, minimalwellness vibe.

The Vision

A calmer, more comprehensive workplace wellness brand, rebuilt and re-platformed without losing a single point of search visibility.

Role
Marketing Executive
Duration
Ongoing

"A growing, more comprehensive workplace wellness and safety compliance offering, not just a simple service page."

AnjouHealth, the sister brand to Physio & Sole, needed a website and content presence that matched where the brand was actually headed: BizSafe certifications, WSH programmes, corporate wellness.

The Challenge
Rebuilding without losing search visibility.

Two revamps, back to back: a business-driven redesign, then a full technical migration off WordPress. The risk with any platform migration is losing rankings built up over years in the process.

1Reflecting an expanding service lineup without a full rebuild each time
2Migrating platforms without a single point of SEO disruption
3Mapping content to a niche B2B compliance audience, not consumers
What I did
Two Site Revamps

The first rebuilt the site for the brand's expanding lineup; the second was a full migration off WordPress onto a modern AI-native stack.

SEO Strategy

Mapped content and keywords to real search demand: BizSafe levels, WSH officer requirements, toolbox meetings, ergonomics.

Cross-Brand & Events

Managed cross-promotion with Physio & Sole and handled event support, translating WSH content for on-the-ground activations.

Proof point: organic growth through two revamps

Organic performance was flat and modest for months (22 to 56 clicks per month between August 2025 and February 2026), then took off the month the redesign shipped. Clicks climbed steadily from 100 (March) to 666 (July), roughly a 12x increase from the pre-redesign baseline to peak. Crucially, the July AI migration didn't cause an SEO dip: July was actually the single best month on record, and August (even as a partial month) is already tracking close behind. That's real evidence the technical migration was executed cleanly without sacrificing search visibility.

What's working

The strongest content is BizSafe certification guides (Levels 1 through 4), workplace safety officer and WSH-officer-vs-safety-coordinator content, toolbox meeting guides, and ergonomics content, all mapped directly to what companies are actually searching for when they need to get compliant or run a wellness programme. One guide even ranks well in Chinese, translated OSH certification content aimed at a different language audience.

Traffic skews desktop (65% vs. 35% mobile), which tracks with a B2B audience researching compliance topics during work hours, a different pattern than Physio & Sole's mobile-heavy consumer traffic, and one that shaped how I think about page layout and content length for this brand specifically.

Corporate wellness event campaigns

Beyond the website, I lead design and content for AnjouHealth's onsite wellness event campaigns for clients including Google, Collins, DHL, and SAESL, all ongoing, recurring engagements rather than one-off projects. Collins, DHL, and SAESL run in Singapore, while the Google programme spans multiple markets: Singapore, Malaysia, Taiwan, Japan, and Thailand.

For each event I design the full suite of physical and digital materials: posters, merch (t-shirts, stickers), and interactive activity cards used on the day. I also film and edit the post-event recap content, and from time to time I step in as the presenter/host at the event itself. I've also had the chance to travel for this, including an onsite activation in Taiwan for the Google programme.

Results
  • Organic clicks grew roughly 12x from pre-redesign baseline to peak month (July 2026).
  • Successfully migrated the site off WordPress onto a modern AI-native stack with zero SEO disruption. Traffic kept climbing through and after the migration.
  • Built out a full library of ranking content across BizSafe certification tiers and WSH compliance topics.
  • Organic Search is the second-largest traffic channel (37.4% of sessions) and has the strongest engagement rate of any channel except Referral, 55.2%, well ahead of Direct traffic's 20%, meaning organic visitors are far more likely to actually engage once they land.
  • Lead designer and on-the-ground content producer for recurring enterprise wellness programmes with Google (multi-market: SG, MY, TW, JP, TH), Collins, DHL, and SAESL.

The Vision

Build real anticipation for a new outlet before the doors even open, and make the brand instantly recognisable across every platform.

Case Study · Jarrons & Co Jan 2024

A warehouse salethat sold itself.

The Vision

Turn curiosity into reach: a guessing-game teaser campaign for a brand collaboration, engineered so every action compounded the next.

Role
Freelance Social Media Marketer
Duration
Jan 2024

"Teaser posts invited followers to guess the collaboration partner before it was revealed."

I led the marketing for a collaborative warehouse sale between Jarrons & Co and Baa Baa Sheepz, a local brand well known for its baby products. The approach centred on a guessing game featuring the Baa Baa Sheepz mascot, alongside sneak peeks of sale items and their discounted prices.

The Approach
A giveaway built on compounding actions.

To convert curiosity into reach, I ran a giveaway where each step extended the campaign to a new audience, on top of location tags, event details, and relevant hashtags to reach people who hadn't heard of either brand yet.

1Following both brands' accounts
2Sharing the post to a story
3Commenting a set phrase while tagging friends

Growth across every metric that matters.
